What 20 Years of Producing Events Taught Me About Community

November 20, 2026 · 1 min read

This is a sample post so you can see the layout. Replace it with your own writing.

After more than 200 events, from 50-person startup lounges to 10,000-attendee festivals, one lesson keeps coming back: people don’t come for the agenda. They come for each other.

Design for the hallway

The most valuable moments at a conference rarely happen on stage. They happen in line for coffee, at the edge of the expo floor, or in the ten minutes between sessions. Good programming gives people a reason to show up. Good design gives them a reason to talk.

  • Build in longer breaks than feels comfortable
  • Put seating where conversations naturally start
  • Give attendees a simple, shared prompt to break the ice

Small rooms, big impact

Some of the best sessions I’ve produced had 40 people in the room, not 400. When the audience can ask real questions, everyone leaves with something useful.

“The conference is the excuse. The community is the point.”

What’s next

That thinking is a big part of why we built Levelge: to keep those connections going long after the event ends.
